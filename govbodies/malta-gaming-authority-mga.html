Role & Objective

The Malta Gaming Authority is the single regulatory body responsible for the governance of all gaming activities in Malta, spanning land-based and remote (online/iGaming) gaming. It is one of the largest and longest-established gaming regulators in the EU, and Malta's iGaming sector is a significant contributor to the national economy.
The MGA's core functions include:
  • Licensing & supervision — authorising and supervising gaming operators, assessing compliance costs and reporting requirements, and enforcing the Gaming Act and its regulatory instruments;
  • Player protection — placing responsible gaming and player protection at the top of its regulatory agenda, including oversight of commercial communications and advertising standards;
  • Anti-money laundering — maintaining an AML/CFT framework aligned with national and international standards, via a dedicated AML Steering Committee;
  • Fit-and-proper screening — assessing licence applicants and existing licensees on criminal-probity and other fitness grounds via a dedicated Fit and Proper Committee;
  • Technology & data governance — overseeing the Authority's own IT posture, cybersecurity resilience, and data governance following formalisation of an IT/Data Governance framework in 2024;
  • ESG — since 2023, developing and implementing the Authority's own Environmental, Social and Governance strategy.
Governance is unusually multi-layered for a Maltese authority: a Board of Governors (appointed by the Minister responsible for gaming, overseeing strategy and policy) sits above an Executive Committee (day-to-day operations), with additional standing bodies including an Audit Committee, Supervisory Council, ESG Committee, Commercial Communications Committee, Compliance & Enforcement Committee, Fit and Proper Committee, IT and Data Governance Committee, and AML Steering Committee.
