Role & Objective

The Malta Communications Authority is the statutory regulator for Malta's electronic communications sectors — fixed and mobile telephony, internet and TV distribution services — as well as postal services and the eCommerce sector. Its mandate derives from the Malta Communications Authority Act (Cap 418), and it also implements the Electronic Communications (Regulations) Act, the Postal Services Act, and the Electronic Commerce Act.
The MCA's mission rests on two pillars:
  • to promote and safeguard sustainable competition, customer choice and value for money in the electronic communications, e-commerce and postal sectors; and
  • to facilitate an environment conducive to investment, innovation, social inclusion and economic growth.
In practice this covers: enabling market entry through a light-touch authorisation framework for networks and services; regulating network access to ensure effective end-user choice; overseeing MaltaPost's Universal Service Obligation and cost-based pricing; supporting eCommerce take-up by local businesses; running digital-inclusion initiatives aimed at ensuring every business and individual has the skills to engage online; and, more recently, exploring new economic opportunities linked to its regulatory portfolio.
The Authority's regulatory remit has expanded significantly with EU digital-policy developments: the MCA now also administers obligations under the Digital Services Act, and — as confirmed by the current Chairperson in the 2024 Annual Report — has been designated as the competent authority under Malta's NIS2 transposition (Legal Notice 71 of 2025) for the digital infrastructure and postal/courier sectors, giving it a direct cybersecurity-supervision role alongside its traditional telecoms and postal remit.
Governance sits with a Board of Directors, fully reconstituted on 7 November 2024, replacing the previous board chaired by Mark Musù. The current Chairperson, Inġ. Jonathan Scerri, an engineer by qualification, has described the Board's focus as strategic direction, good governance, and supporting the Authority through its expanding regulatory mandate.

NIS2 Competent-Authority Supervision Support
Cyber — Cyber Defence & Resilience
As the designated NIS2 competent authority for digital infrastructure and postal/courier sectors, the MCA now supervises essential and important entities' cybersecurity compliance. Deloitte's Cyber Defence & Resilience and Cyber Strategy & Transformation capabilities could support the Authority in developing supervisory frameworks, incident-response coordination protocols, or capacity-building for its expanded NIS2 role.
Digital Services Act Compliance Advisory
Risk, Regulatory & Forensic
The MCA's regulatory mandate now extends to the Digital Services Act. Deloitte's regulatory advisory practice could support impact assessments, guidance development, or compliance-monitoring frameworks as the MCA builds out its DSA supervisory function alongside its established telecoms remit.
Postal Universal Service Obligation & Cost-Modelling Review
Strategy & Transactions — Infrastructure & Real Estate
The MCA is responsible for ensuring MaltaPost's Universal Service Obligation is maintained and that pricing is cost-geared. Deloitte's infrastructure and cost-modelling expertise could support periodic USO cost reviews or benchmarking against comparable EU postal regulators.
eCommerce & Digital Economy Strategy Support
Customer — Marketing, Commerce & Product / Strategy & Business Design
The MCA's eCommerce mandate includes implementing initiatives under Malta's national eCommerce strategy. Deloitte's digital-commerce and business-design capability could support market studies or strategy refresh work as the Authority updates its approach to supporting local eCommerce take-up.
Telecoms Market & Quality-of-Service Benchmarking
Enterprise Technology & Performance
The MCA runs independent Quality of Service benchmarking for mobile communications (its second such benchmark concluded in 2025). Deloitte's technology-performance and telecoms-sector benchmarking experience could support methodology design or comparative EU-market analysis for future benchmarking rounds.
