Role & Objective

The Malta Digital Innovation Authority is the government authority that directs and facilitates the secure and optimal uptake of digital innovation in Malta. Its core mission is fostering trust in innovative technologies — building regulatory and technical frameworks that let AI, distributed ledger technology (DLT), and data-driven services grow in Malta while managing the associated risks.
MDIA's mandate spans several distinct functions:
  • AI policy & readiness — MDIA owns Malta's national AI strategy (originally launched in 2019, one of the first in the world, currently being realigned for 2025 with societal well-being and sustainability at its core) and publishes EU AI Act self-assessment tools (an "AI Classification Guide" and "AI Compliance Role Finder") to help organisations navigate incoming EU regulation;
  • Cybersecurity — MDIA is Malta's National Cybersecurity Certification Authority (NCCA), with both supervisory and certification functions, and runs the National Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure Policy;
  • Data governance — under the EU Data Governance Act, MDIA is the designated competent authority for data altruism organisations and data intermediation services;
  • DLT/Blockchain — MDIA participates in the European Blockchain Regulatory Sandbox and leads Malta's role in the European Blockchain Partnership;
  • Digital literacy & talent — MDIA advises government on digital-skills policy, helps expand ICT educational programmes, leads an ICT-professionalism development programme, and runs scholarships (Pathfinder Digital Scholarship, recently relaunched with a €150,000 fund) and youth outreach (summer schools, bootcamps);
  • Innovation ecosystem support — MDIA spearheads DiHubMT, Malta's European Digital Innovation Hub, a one-stop-shop supporting start-ups, SMEs and enterprises through digital transformation as part of the EU-wide EDIH network; it also runs the MDIA Applied Research Grant (MARG) and a Technology Sandbox for testing new solutions against recognised standards.
Governance is split between an Executive/Top Management team handling day-to-day operations (led by CEO Kenneth Brincat, with a Chief Innovative Technology Officer and Chief Strategy, Policy & Governance Officer) and a Board of Governors chaired by Wayne Grixti, who is concurrently CEO of Tech.mt — reflecting close institutional overlap between Malta's two main digital-economy bodies.

Related Deloitte Experience

EU Artificial Intelligence Act — A new era for AI regulation
Deloitte Malta — Regulatory Perspective
Deloitte Malta's Audit & Assurance practice published an early explainer on the proposed EU AI Act, noting it would apply to all providers, importers, distributors and users of AI systems affecting people in the EU regardless of where the organisation is established, and that organisations would need to re-examine and enhance control frameworks to meet its requirements. This mirrors the exact regulatory space MDIA operates in as it builds Malta's own EU AI Act self-assessment tools.

Data governance and AI readiness
Deloitte Malta — Technology Perspective, Mar 2025
Deloitte Malta published practical guidance on preparing organisations for AI, arguing that organisations succeeding with AI are those that fix their data governance foundations first — covering data ownership, unified taxonomy, IT controls, and a maturity curve from descriptive to predictive analytics. This closely parallels MDIA's own national role in setting AI readiness expectations for Maltese organisations.
