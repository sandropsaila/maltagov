Role & Objective

Malta Freeport Corporation Ltd — also known as the Malta Freeport Authority — is the official state entity responsible for regulating and supporting operations within Malta's Freeport area at Marsaxlokk, one of the Mediterranean's leading transhipment hubs. Established under the Malta Freeports Act (1989), the Corporation acts as both landlord and regulator, managing the land and infrastructure while ensuring that activities within the Freeport are conducted responsibly, efficiently, and in the national interest.
The Corporation's stated mission is to support and regulate Malta's Freeport activities in a manner that fosters economic growth, operational excellence, and environmental sustainability. Its role spans three areas:
  • Regulatory Oversight — overseeing activities of the private-sector operators and stakeholders within the Freeport zone;
  • Security — safeguarding the Freeport area;
  • Strategic Development — planning the long-term infrastructure and business development of the zone.
Operationally, the Freeport is a landlord-port model: the Corporation owns and regulates the land and infrastructure, while operations are carried out by private stakeholders including Malta Freeport Terminals (the privatised container-terminal operator, majority-owned by Yildirim Group's Yilport with the remainder held via Terminal Link between CMA CGM and China Merchants Port Holdings), Evos Malta, MedservRegis, and Malta Fish Farms. The Corporation is currently navigating the impact of EU Emissions Trading Scheme (ETS) port charges and Red Sea/Suez Canal disruption on Mediterranean transhipment competitiveness, while also engaging in Mediterranean port-sector diplomacy — CEO David Magro was elected President of the MEDPorts Association in 2026.
