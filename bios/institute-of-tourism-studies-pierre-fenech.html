Pierre Fenech

Chief Executive Officer — Institute of Tourism Studies (ITS)
Ministry for Tourism (MFT) · Formerly also CEO, Mediterranean Conference Centre (MCC), 2015–2025

Overview

Pierre Fenech is the Chief Executive Officer of the Institute of Tourism Studies (ITS), Malta's state tourism and hospitality education institution. He built his career in hotel and catering management before moving into the public sector, where for nearly a decade he simultaneously held two government CEO posts — ITS and the Mediterranean Conference Centre (MCC) — a dual arrangement that earned him the nickname "Super CEO" in the local press. He relinquished the MCC role in early 2025 and now focuses solely on ITS.

Career & background

Fenech began his career in food and beverage, event and general management roles at establishments including the Bugibba Holiday Complex, The Coastline Hotel and Island Caterers. In 2008 he was appointed Director of Operations and Quality Assurance at Island Caterers, a post he held until November 2012, when he became General Manager of MKIC Ltd, the company that runs Montekristo Estates on behalf of Island Hotels Group.

He is an ITS graduate himself, holding a Diploma in Hospitality Management from the Institute, followed by a B.Com (Hons) in Tourism from the University of Malta and an Executive MBA. He has also lectured on tourism and hospitality subjects.

In March 2015 he was appointed CEO of the Mediterranean Conference Centre, Malta's largest conference venue, housed in a 16th-century building in Valletta. He later also took on the CEO role at ITS, holding both positions concurrently for years — a unique arrangement among Maltese government appointees. In March 2025, Tourism Minister Ian Borg did not renew his MCC contract; Nigel Vella was appointed to succeed him at the MCC from April 2025, and Fenech continued solely as CEO of ITS.

Controversies

In 2019, Fenech gave Labour MP Rosianne Cutajar a paid consultancy contract that was later described as a "phantom" job, since she was found to have done little to no work for the payment. A probe by the Commissioner for Standards in Public Life questioned the ethics of the arrangement but did not find Fenech personally at fault, since the tourism ministry's then permanent secretary had approved the related expenses; that permanent secretary was subsequently removed from the post.

In 2021, Fenech issued a direct order using ITS funds to architect Gilbert Bartolo, who was the brother of then-Tourism Minister Clayton Bartolo. Fenech has said he was not aware of the family relationship at the time the order was placed.
